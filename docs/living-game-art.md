# Animated combat effects

Generated with the built-in image generation tool. The three original 4 × 4 transparent sheets are packed into padded 1024 × 1024 WebP atlases (16 frames, 256 pixels per cell). Runtime frame blending uses the cached sheets and a bounded bank of 128 immutable blended cells with 120 animation steps, avoiding repeated writes to a shared burst canvas during crowded combat; no simulation state or RNG is changed.

## fire

Asset: `public/art/fire-burst.webp`

Exact prompt:

Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: a production fantasy action RPG VFX animation sprite sheet. Primary request: A powerful orange and gold fire explosion: expanding rolling flames, bright molten core, turbulent flame tongues and embers, then fading dark smoke. Rich hand-painted flame texture with cinematic volume and light. Create EXACTLY 16 consecutive animation frames in an EXACT 4-column by 4-row square grid, read left to right and top to bottom. All 16 equal square cells have transparent backgrounds and no drawn grid lines. The same effect is anchored at the EXACT center of every cell, viewed slightly from above, orthographic, meant to overlay ground in a 2D game. Frame 1 a tiny dim gathering spark, frames 2-4 rapid bright ignition, frames 5-8 powerful spreading explosion, frames 9-12 spreading trails and dissolving smoke, frames 13-16 soft sparse nearly faded wisps. Each full effect stays ENTIRELY INSIDE its own cell with at least 12 percent transparent padding to every cell edge, never crosses cell boundaries. Consistent scale and camera for all cells; actual changing geometry and volume as the effect evolves. Premium detailed painted fantasy game art with crisp shapes and soft translucent volumetric effects, complementary to painted Warcraft-style fantasy sprites; not flat vector graphics. Truly transparent alpha everywhere outside the effects, no black or white background, no characters, no environment, no labels, no numbers, no border, no watermark.

The gameplay renderer retains the original painted hero, companion, shapeshift, mount and creature atlases. Animation rotates weighted texture joints around shoulders, elbows, hips, knees, paws, heads and tails. Front/back weapon placement follows the original illustrations. Heroes and companions interpolate mesh vertices continuously instead of crossfading whole images, preserving opaque bodies and single weapon outlines. Walking legs remain active under upper-body casts; start/stop transitions settle into breathing, cloth, wing and head motion. Attacks follow their accepted release event, while boss wind-ups follow actual warning duration. Casting light follows the animated hand or weapon; mounted riders retain head, arm and cloth motion on a separate texture surface, clipped at the waist, while moving with their painted mount. Live skinning reuses each actor’s canvas with a six-skin budget per render. Ordinary crowds retain bounded shared gait frames. Evolved summons use the painted Infernal, Fire Elemental and Wrath Wolf artwork in gameplay as well as the spellbook.

Presentation state remains separate from gameplay state, collisions, damage, progression and RNG. Interpolated positions, camera follow, hit recoil, footfalls and spell-school effects animate around the original artwork. Motion settings and pause apply to both character animation and effects.

The game shell suppresses native scrollbar paint for every game element while preserving wheel/touch/keyboard/controller reading. Native select fields retain their semantics and change handlers; `game-picker.ts` supplies a paged pointer/Enter popup with 44px choices. The picker works inside inert-backed dialogs, closes on Escape/Back/outside click, and preserves controller focus within its panel.
