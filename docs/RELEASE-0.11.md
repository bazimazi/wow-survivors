# Release 0.11 — profession quests and mastery

## Design before implementation

Add optional, shared quest chains for all nine primary professions and three secondary trades. Each has four ordered projects at skill 1/50/125/225, character level 1/5/10/20, and trained ranks Apprentice/Journeyman/Expert/Artisan. Training remains available independently. Accept a project explicitly; only future successful actions count. Projects accumulate across heroes and returns, with eligibility checked for the acting hero. Each turn-in consumes a clearly displayed quantity of that project's material grade and grants gold and selected-hero XP. The last project also requires skill 300 and awards a permanent level-20 epic mastery trinket. No existing stock, skill or history completes an unaccepted project.

Gathering projects count directly gathered material units of the exact grade in named destinations. Herbalism explores Elwynn/Elwynn/Westfall/Tirisfal; Mining explores Elwynn/Westfall/Westfall-or-dungeons/Tirisfal; Skinning follows wolves in Elwynn/Westfall/Westfall/Elwynn-or-Westfall; Fishing visits Elwynn/Westfall/Deadmines/Tirisfal. Cloth, caches, quest rewards and recycled gear do not count as gathering. Crafting projects count successful transactions with recipes of the exact required grade. Enchanting also counts successful equipment formula applications in all four grades. Expert Engineering uses an existing skill-150 specialization recipe; Expert Blacksmithing and Leatherworking use specialization work or learned skill-125 faction patterns. These projects retain the existing recipe gates. Alchemy and First Aid additionally test healing supplies on an injured hero, Engineering tests bombs that actually damage enemies, and Cooking requires completing a minute of an expedition with a consumed meal. Supply inventories remain shared; the field tests do not claim item-by-item provenance.

Gathering goals are 8/10/12/16 units; crafting goals are 2/3/4/6 transactions. Material deliveries cost 2/3/4/6 units. Gold rewards are 30/60/90/150 and XP rewards 100/200/350/600. Field healing goals are 1/2/3/4, bomb goals 1/1/2/3 and meals 1/1/2/2. Final mastery equipment is shared and protected from selling/disenchanting. Existing equipment, trainers, specializations, recipes and formulas keep their rules.

Each accepted project has a bounded unique attempt token. Expedition snapshots and returned evidence include that token, project and trade. Duplicate settlement, previous attempts, abandoned projects and earlier chapters cannot grant credit. Abandoning or forgetting a primary trade clears its unfinished progress and token, while claimed chapters and mastery rewards remain. Relearning requires meeting the current project's gates again. Old version-1 saves begin with twelve unaccepted chains and retain all previous progress; malformed fields and evidence are bounded and allowlisted.

The Professions page has a guild workbench with known/all/single-trade filtering, a four-rank timeline, current objectives, exact delivery costs, eligibility and a final reward preview. Camp and navigation show active/ready work; pause previews this run's unsaved contribution and results show saved progress. Mobile controls have usable touch sizes and no horizontal overflow. Existing illustration assets and vector icons suffice for this phase.

## Research

The [Classic primer](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/23090134/wow-classic-primer-for-new-players) confirms two primary professions and three secondary trades. [Triage's original quest text](https://classicdb.ch/?quest=6624) tests successful treatment of injured patients; [Clamlette Surprise](https://classicdb.ch/?quest=6610) exchanges collected ingredients for cooking instruction. The [Classic Fishing/Cooking guide](https://www.wow-professions.com/classic/fishing-and-cooking-leveling-guide-classic-wow) describes Nat Pagle's travel-and-catch quest; the [Classic First Aid guide](https://www.wow-professions.com/classic/first-aid-leveling-guide-classic-wow) connects Artisan training to Triage. Original database and modern Classic guides differ on some entry-level metadata, so this adaptation relies on the activity structure and uses explicitly original compressed gates. Modern expansion and Season of Discovery profession changes are excluded.

The twelve guild mentors, forty-eight projects, field-test requirements, fees/rewards and mastery trinkets are original designs. They do not reproduce the original patient minigame, rare quest-fish items, NPC locations, or a complete vanilla quest catalog.

## Implementation and verification

The guild workbench, filters, rank timelines, exact delivery/reward previews, acceptance and abandonment, review dialogs, camp notice, navigation badge and pending/saved expedition status are implemented. Save version 1 retains old progress and starts old players with unaccepted profession projects. Attempt snapshots prevent stale or abandoned evidence from granting credit. Mastery rewards are shared, require level 20 to equip and are protected from selling and disenchanting. The equipment catalog now contains 114 items; the existing 55 recipes and twelve equipment formulas keep their costs and eligibility rules.

The complete suite passes: **166 simulation/progression tests and 63 browser tests**, plus strict TypeScript checking, the production build and formatting. Twenty-three new simulation/progression checks cover all forty-eight ordered claims with real crafting transactions, all-class equipment eligibility, final skill and delivery gates, atomic rejection, all four equipment-enchant grades, real node gathering and skinning, cache/cloth exclusions, travel, actual healing and bomb damage, meal clocks, independent snapshots, stale and duplicate returns, bounded imports, migration and permanent ownership. An Expert Fishing check reaches the third room twice through actual guardian defeat/recovery and collects six trout per visit. An automatic-combat comparison confirms mastery bonuses affect damage, and a catalog check prevents any mastery item being strictly dominated by an earlier shared trinket.

Seven browser flows pass, covering old saves and filters, real collection and return, unsaved pause versus saved result status, reviewed exact deductions, actual future crafts and healing, consumed meals and paused time, a final Artisan craft reaching 300 before mastery claim/equipment/shared use, abandon/unlearn consequences and six viewport widths. Desktop and phone captures are produced by `node scripts/capture-guild.mjs`; each capture uses an isolated save.

The original five combat atlases decode successfully. Existing 400-enemy scenes stay bounded: outdoor average/p95 CPU time 1.56/2.4 ms, Deadmines 1.32/1.7 ms and Ragefire 1.27/1.6 ms. These local simulation/drawing measurements exclude presentation and do not estimate performance on other hardware. Base encounter statistics were not retuned.

## Remaining evaluation

Human playtesting is needed for project duration, material delivery costs, specialization/faction acquisition and mastery-build balance. The current chains are finite optional goals, rather than a complete vanilla profession quest catalog. Deadmines visits and later outdoor gathering give the existing map progression a purpose; new zones and additional profession adventures remain future content.
